写作教会你如何思考、如何快速掌握任何技能，以及如何吸引一群支持你作品的受众。如果你想创办自己的事业，或只是想从事任何形式的独立创作或有意义的工作，这些能力都是绝对必要的。

在 AI 时代，思考、学习和分发（把你的价值呈现在他人面前，让自己真正有机会获得报酬）都是永不过时的技能。写作让你能够同时练习这三项技能，并在此过程中顺带构建数字杠杆。

我从没想过自己会成为一名作家。

无论从哪个角度看，写作作为一种简单的晨间习惯，都彻底改变了我的思想与人生轨迹。它帮助我清晰表达自己的想法、平复内心、找到传统就业市场之外的机会；它也是推动我的工作和事业取得成功的唯一核心动力。

在这封信中，我想向你展示我捕捉想法、写出值得一读的内容所用的系统；但我也想告诉你，为什么这条路如今比以往任何时候都更可行，以及它为什么会改变你的大脑与现实互动的方式。

没错，就是这么深刻。

让我们开始吧。

## 旧杠杆与新杠杆：现代成功的秘密 / Old leverage VS new leverage: The secret to modern success

> **EN:** The biggest leverage is no longer capital. The biggest leverage is enterprise — the ability to run a great business. And in particular, the biggest leverage is personal brand. Having a big audience. As soon as you’ve got people paying attention to you, you have this enormous form of leverage.
>
> – Daniel Priestley

> 最大的杠杆不再是资本。最大的杠杆是创业经营能力——把一家企业经营好的能力。尤其重要的是，最大的杠杆是个人品牌，是拥有庞大的受众。一旦有人开始关注你，你就拥有了这种巨大的杠杆。
>
> ——Daniel Priestley

> **EN:** The rules of wealth creation have fundamentally changed.

创造财富的规则已经发生了根本性变化。

> **EN:** The assets that drove income in the Industrial Age (property, factories, machinery, physical goods) are being replaced by digital assets (audience, personal brand, IP, content, data, software) that are incredibly cheap to build, more scalable, harder to tax, and compound while you sleep.

工业时代驱动收入的资产（房产、工厂、机器、实物商品）正被数字资产（受众、个人品牌、知识产权、内容、数据、软件）取代。构建这些数字资产的成本低得惊人，它们更容易规模化、更难被征税，而且在你睡觉时也能产生复利。

> **EN:** The only requirement to building leverage for yourself in today’s world is a good idea and the confidence to post it on the internet.

在当今世界，为自己构建杠杆唯一需要的，就是一个好想法，以及把它发布到互联网上的信心。

> **EN:** The problem, and opportunity, is that most people are still conditioned by Industrial values. They somehow still believe that school → job → retire is the most certain, safe, and secure when that couldn’t be any further from the truth.

问题，同时也是机会，在于大多数人仍受工业时代价值观的塑造。不知为何，他们依旧认为“上学 → 工作 → 退休”是最确定、最安全、最稳妥的道路，而事实与此相去甚远。

> **EN:** The same holds true when it comes to building leverage, and thus creating wealth, for your future.

在为自己的未来构建杠杆、进而创造财富这件事上，道理同样如此。

> **EN:** Old leverage comes from physical, tangible assets.

旧杠杆来自有形的实体资产。

> **EN:** A house, factory, Rolex, fleet of trucks, warehouse of inventory, gold. You buy them, then let them appreciate or generate income.

房子、工厂、劳力士手表、卡车车队、仓库里的存货、黄金。你买下它们，然后让它们升值或产生收入。

> **EN:** Old leverage is linear and local.

旧杠杆是线性的，也是局部受限的。

> **EN:** A factory can only produce so many units per day, and a house can only be rented to one tenant at a time.

一家工厂每天只能生产有限数量的产品，一套房子同一时间也只能租给一位租客。

> **EN:** Don’t get me wrong, leverage is leverage, and old leverage is still a great thing.

别误会我的意思，杠杆就是杠杆，旧杠杆依然是很有价值的东西。

> **EN:** However, physical assets are going to be the first thing that governments find, tax, and seize if wages collapse, which is more likely today than yesterday because AI can swiftly do that, leading to governments expanding social safety nets.

然而，如果工资体系崩溃，实体资产将会是政府最先找到、征税和没收的东西。如今这种崩溃比过去更有可能发生，因为 AI 能迅速造成这种局面，进而促使政府扩大社会安全网。

> **EN:** Governments are much less likely to seize an email list of people who know who you are, a library of content, or a best-selling book.

政府没那么可能没收一份由认识你的人组成的电子邮件名单、一套内容资料库，或一本畅销书。

> **EN:** New leverage comes from intangible, digital assets.

新杠杆来自无形的数字资产。

> **EN:** An audience, personal brand, IP, data, systems, software. These are the leverage of the newly rich. Media, data, and code. Content can be written once and be seen by millions of people. Software can be created once and serve millions of users.

受众、个人品牌、知识产权、数据、系统、软件。这些是新富阶层所运用的杠杆：媒体、数据和代码。内容只需创作一次，就能被数百万人看到；软件只需开发一次，就能服务数百万用户。

> **EN:** For most people, writing is the most accessible and practical form of leverage. You can learn to code, but if you cannot attract people to care about the product you built, then nobody will use or pay for it. Writing is the leverage that unlocks other forms of leverage.

> 对大多数人而言，写作是最容易获得、也最实用的一种杠杆。你可以学习编程，但如果你无法吸引人们关注你开发的产品，就不会有人使用它或为它付费。写作是能够解锁其他杠杆形式的杠杆。

![原文插图](/halo-notes/articles/assets/x-2077513202527773093/inline-1.png)

你不会在资产负债表上看到数字资产，但它们代表了标普 500 指数成分股 90% 的价值。

> **EN:** Kim Kardashian, Taylor Swift, Ryan Reynolds, Mr. Beast, Elon Musk - all have massive audiences and personal brands.

Kim Kardashian、Taylor Swift、Ryan Reynolds、Mr. Beast、Elon Musk——他们都拥有庞大的受众和个人品牌。

> **EN:** But you probably don’t want anywhere near their level of fame, and that’s a great thing, because you don’t need millions of followers to make this work. With 6-12 months of focused effort, you can find yourself in a wildly different life than you are now.

但你大概并不想拥有接近他们那种程度的名气，而这反倒是件好事，因为你不需要数百万粉丝也能让这套方法奏效。只要专注投入 6 到 12 个月，你就可能发现自己的生活已与现在截然不同。

> **EN:** Personally, I replaced my income around 5,000 followers (about 6 months in) because I actually took it seriously. My friend Randy, a fitness coach, was making $100k a month at around 10,000 followers, because he invested in education, studied marketing and sales, and implemented everything as if his life depended on it.

就我个人而言，在拥有约 5,000 名粉丝时（大约开始 6 个月后），我就用这份收入取代了原本的收入，因为我确实认真对待这件事。我的朋友 Randy 是一名健身教练，他在约有 10,000 名粉丝时，每月能赚 10 万美元，因为他投资于教育，学习市场营销与销售，并像性命攸关一样落实所学的一切。

> **EN:** The point is that people drastically overestimate how much they need to win, because all they see is out-of-touch celebrities on their timeline.

关键在于，人们严重高估了自己取得成功所需的条件，因为他们在时间线上看到的尽是那些与普通人生活脱节的名人。

> **EN:** That’s why we teach how to find what you want to talk about, create your ‘personal monopoly,’ and [write high-impact content in our bootcamp.](https://eden.so/bootcamp) You still have time to join the next live call we’re doing.

正因如此，我们才会在训练营中教授如何找到你想谈论的主题、打造你的“个人垄断”，以及写出高影响力的内容。你现在仍有时间加入我们下一场直播通话。

> **EN:** But the benefits of writing stem far beyond that.

但写作带来的益处远不止于此。

## 写作如何彻底改变你的大脑与人生 / How writing completely shifts your brain & life

> **EN:** How do I know what I think until I see what I say?
>
> – E.M. Forster

> 我怎么知道自己在想什么，除非我看到自己说了什么？
>
> ——E.M. 福斯特

> **EN:** It’s hard to explain... but the act of writing is much deeper than putting pen to paper.

这很难解释……但写作这一行为，远不只是落笔于纸。

> **EN:** It shifts your mind to see the world in a more meaningful way.

它会改变你的思维，让你以一种更有意义的方式看待世界。

> **EN:** Conversations become a source of inspiration. Your thoughts become filled with ideas you’re wrestling to understand. You notice more detail in life rather than living on the stressful surface.

对话会成为灵感的来源。你的思绪里充满了那些你正努力想要理解的想法。你开始留意生活中更多的细节，而不是只活在充满压力的表层。

> **EN:** In my eyes, there are 4 key benefits to writing:

在我看来，写作有 4 个关键益处：

> **EN:** 1) Writing rewires how you think

1）写作重塑你的思维方式

> **EN:** The core mechanism here is forced linearity.

这里的核心机制是强制线性化。

> **EN:** Which is a fancy way of saying that you can feel like you understand something in your head, but writing forces you to collapse it into a single stream.

说得通俗些，你可能觉得自己在脑中理解了某件事，但写作会迫使你把它压缩成一条单一的思路。

> **EN:** Thinking without writing is like walking through a forest where you can see every direction, while writing itself is like driving on a road through the disorienting mess. You have to decide where you’re going, which turns you’re going to take, whether or not you want to take the scenic route, and how to navigate back if you get lost.

不写下来时的思考，就像走在一片能看向四面八方的森林里；而写作本身，则像在这片令人迷失的混乱中沿着一条道路开车。你必须决定要去哪里、在哪些地方转弯、是否要走风景更美的路线，以及迷路后该如何返回。

> **EN:** In other words, writing is the apparatus for thinking. It’s the gym where your mind is built.

换句话说，写作是用来思考的器械。它是锻造心智的健身房。

> **EN:** You may think you understand the feelings, counterarguments, memories, or half-formed analogies in your head, and even your values and beliefs, but unless you write them out, I can almost guarantee you don’t.

你可能以为自己理解了脑海中的感受、反驳、记忆或尚未成形的类比，甚至自己的价值观与信念；但除非把它们写出来，否则我几乎可以保证，你并不理解。

> **EN:** 2) Writing increases pattern recognition

2）写作提升模式识别能力

> **EN:** There are a few layers to this.

这其中有几个层次。

> **EN:** The first is that by writing regularly, you build an external record of your thinking. You are able to quickly reference what you’ve written in the past as a way or articulating your current thoughts better. It helps you think with clarity.

第一，定期写作会为你的思考建立一份外部记录。你可以迅速回顾过去写过的内容，以此更好地表达当下的想法。它能帮助你清晰地思考。

> **EN:** The second layer is similar to the “photographer’s eye.” After a few years of serious practice, you start to see frames and compositions in everyday life. The world doesn’t look the same, it has another layer of depth.

第二个层次类似于“摄影师之眼”。经过几年认真练习后，你会开始在日常生活中看到画面与构图。世界看起来不再一样，它多了一层深度。

> **EN:** With writing, you start noticing structure in an argument, a metaphor in a mundane moment, or the narrative arc of your average day. Writing trains a specific kind of attention that bleeds into everything.

写作会让你开始留意论证的结构、平凡时刻里的隐喻，或普通一天的叙事弧线。写作训练的是一种特定的注意力，而这种注意力会渗入一切。

> **EN:** The third layer is that you notice ideas you normally wouldn’t have.

第三个层次是，你会注意到那些通常不会注意到的想法。

> **EN:** When I have a newsletter planned for the week, or just know that I need to write a tweet today, my mind will naturally help me notice ideas that help achieve those goals.

当我已经规划好这周的通讯内容，或者只是知道自己今天需要写一条推文时，我的大脑自然会帮我留意那些有助于实现这些目标的想法。

> **EN:** It’s the concept of psycho-cybernetics - that your mind is like a heat seeking missile towards it’s goals, and will help you notice the tools you need to achieve them (the problem is that people don’t actually have conscious goals they care about, they have unconscious goals assigned to them, and since their path is planned for them, they don’t have to do much aside from show up to school and work, so they are missing this layer of life).

这就是心理控制论的概念——你的大脑就像一枚追踪目标的热寻导弹，会帮助你注意到实现目标所需的工具（问题在于，人们其实没有自己真正在乎的自觉目标；他们只有被赋予的无意识目标。由于道路已经替他们规划好，除了去学校和上班报到之外，他们不必做太多，因此错失了生活的这一层）。

> **EN:** 3) Writing helps you articulate your thoughts

3）写作帮助你清晰表达自己的想法

> **EN:** Most people walk around believing they understand things they don’t.

大多数人四处生活着，深信自己理解了那些其实并不理解的事。

> **EN:** Not because they’re dumb, but because they’ve never been forced to externalize the thought and look at it. Writing is not only a tool to improve communication - thus improving most other areas of your life - but a tool to expose whether or not you actually know what you’re talking about.

这不是因为他们愚笨，而是因为他们从未被迫把想法外化出来并审视它。写作不仅是一种改善沟通的工具——从而改善你生活中的大多数其他方面——也是一种揭示你是否真的知道自己在说什么的工具。

> **EN:** That’s what most people are afraid of.

这正是大多数人害怕的事。

> **EN:** They don’t want to feel the struggle that comes from doing something meaningful. They want it to be easy. They want all the ideas to just come to their head immediately. They see writing as something that everyone knows how to do, so they don’t appreciate that it is a skill just like anything else, and takes effort to improve.

他们不想感受做有意义之事所带来的挣扎。他们希望一切轻而易举，希望所有想法都能立刻涌入脑海。他们把写作看成人人都会做的事，因此没有意识到它和其他任何事情一样是一项技能，也需要付出努力才能进步。

> **EN:** Which leads into the most important point.

这就引出了最重要的一点。

> **EN:** 4) Writing helps you learn faster

4）写作帮助你更快地学习

> **EN:** The future belongs to those who can learn the fastest.

未来属于那些学习速度最快的人。

> **EN:** And unfortunately, most people stop learning after they graduate high school, and it shows.

遗憾的是，大多数人高中毕业后就停止了学习，而且这一点显而易见。

> **EN:** You must learn in order to achieve a goal, so if you want to achieve any goal outside of the three society planted in your head at birth, then you must self-educate, and if you want to enhance that process, you must write.

要实现目标，你就必须学习。因此，如果你想实现社会从你出生起就灌输到你脑中的那三个目标以外的任何目标，你就必须自我教育；而如果你想强化这一过程，你就必须写作。

> **EN:** Writing is both a form of teaching and understanding.

写作既是一种教学方式，也是一种理解方式。

> **EN:** In learning science, there’s the “protégé effect,” which shows that students who teach material retain it better than students who merely study it.

学习科学中有一种“门生效应”，它表明，教授材料的学生比仅仅学习材料的学生记得更牢。

> **EN:** The cognitive load required to figure out how to explain something to someone else forces deeper processing, better organization, and identification of gaps in knowledge.

> 要想弄清楚如何向他人解释某件事，所需的认知负荷会迫使你进行更深入的处理、更好地组织信息，并找出知识中的缺口。

> **EN:** I learned more in my first 6 months of writing online than I ever did in school. I’m exaggerating, but what I mean is that I learned much more about the things I care about. I could feel my mind changing shape, and I was closer to being like the thinkers that I admired as I was growing up. My younger self thought my brain was just too dumb to speak intelligently. Turns out, I just didn’t practice.

开始在网上写作的头 6 个月里，我学到的东西比在学校里学到的还多。我这么说有些夸张，但我的意思是，对于自己在乎的事物，我学到的东西多得多。我能感觉到自己的思维正在改变形状，也越来越接近那些我成长过程中所钦佩的思想家。年少的我以为，自己的大脑就是太笨，无法有见地地说话。事实证明，我只是没有练习。

> **EN:** Last, writing demands that you understand.

最后，写作要求你真正理解。

> **EN:** You read something.

你读到某个东西。

> **EN:** You feel like you get it.

你觉得自己懂了。

> **EN:** You try to write about it.

你试着把它写出来。

> **EN:** You realize you can’t.

你发现自己写不出来。

> **EN:** Most people think it’s because they aren’t “qualified” to write about the topic, which is bullsh*t. You can write about an idea just like any other person on planet Earth can. You’re just creating invisible gatekeepers out of fear of putting your ideas in front of other people.

大多数人以为，这是因为自己没有“资格”写这个主题——纯属胡扯。你当然可以写一个想法，地球上的任何人也都可以。你只不过是害怕把自己的想法摆到别人面前，于是凭空制造出了一群看不见的守门人。

> **EN:** But now you know exactly where your understanding breaks.

但现在，你已经清楚地知道自己的理解是在哪里断裂的。

> **EN:** That’s when you go back and learn. You research. Then you continue writing.

这时，你就回头学习。去研究。然后继续写。

## 我用来产生让人忍不住读下去的想法的写作系统 / The writing system I use to generate ideas people can't help but read

> **EN:** I’ve never been the most organized person.

我从来都不是最有条理的人。

> **EN:** I’ve tried the second brain apps. I’ve tried doing everything inside of AI.

我试过各种第二大脑应用，也试过把所有事情都放到 AI 里完成。

> **EN:** But I always found myself going back to jotting down ideas in a long-running note. Of course, ideas would get lost all the time, but I don’t see that as a problem. If you need to remember it, it’s not important, and if it’s important, you will remember it.

但我发现，自己最后总会回到那份长期维护的笔记里，随手记下想法。当然，想法经常会丢失，但我不觉得这是个问题。如果你还需要刻意记住它，那它就不重要；如果它很重要，你自然会记得。

> **EN:** I’ve found that great writing (or content) that actually gains traction and doesn’t disappear into a black hole of nobody caring about your work boils down to a few things:

我发现，真正能够获得关注、不会掉进无人关心的黑洞里的优秀写作（或内容），归根结底取决于以下几点：

> **EN:** You need ONE topic or theme for the week

- 一周只需要一个主题或主线

> **EN:** You need a place to quickly jot down ideas

- 你需要一个能快速记下想法的地方

> **EN:** You need a way to capture inspiration (social posts, YouTube videos, Substack articles, etc)

- 你需要一种捕捉灵感的方式（社交媒体帖子、YouTube 视频、Substack 文章等）

> **EN:** You need to understand how to package the hook or title so that people actually want to read it

- 你需要懂得如何包装开头的钩子或标题，让人们真正想读

> **EN:** You need to effectively pull from your ideas, inspiration, and research while you are actually writing or creating

- 在实际写作或创作时，你需要有效调用自己的想法、灵感和研究素材

> **EN:** This is what that looks like in the form of a system.

下面就是它以系统形式呈现出来的样子。

> **EN:** It may seem complex, but we’ll recap at the end in a simple way.
