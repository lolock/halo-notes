2025 年 Claude Code 发布时，我们惊讶地发现，许多非技术用户开始尝试使用它。从未打开过终端的人，竟然用它创建能够整理文件夹、去重文件和编写电子表格公式的 agent。

但对其他人来说，终端本身就是障碍。不是每个人都在终端里工作，而且大多数人都不在终端里工作。

于是，2026 年 2 月我们发布了 Claude Cowork——一个面向所有人的 agent。这次没有终端。它是一种 AI agent，运行在用户已经拥有的桌面应用中，因此无论是设计师、项目经理还是运营人员，都会觉得它像自己实际使用的办公软件。

六个月过去，Cowork 正在做 Claude Code 做不到的事：将 agent 能力带给营销、财务、运营、HR、销售和支持团队。个人贡献者和高级领导者都在使用它。

如今，我们回顾用户是如何使用 Cowork 的——他们委托了哪些工作，以及为什么这种模式指向了一个新的工作未来。

<!-- lang:en -->

When we released Claude Code in 2025, we were surprised at how many non-technical users started playing around with it. People who had never opened the terminal before were using it to create agents that organized folders, deduplicated files, and wrote spreadsheet formulas.

But for others, the terminal itself was the barrier. Not everyone works in a terminal, and most people don't.

So in February 2026, we shipped Claude Cowork — an agent for everyone. No terminal this time. An AI agent that feels like the office software designers, program managers, and operations people actually use, because it lives in the desktop app they already have.

Six months in, Cowork is doing something Claude Code couldn't: bringing agentic behavior to marketing, finance, operations, HR, sales, and support teams. Individual contributors and senior leaders alike are using it.

Today we're looking back at how people have used Cowork — what kind of work they've delegated, and why this pattern points to a new future of work.

<!-- /bilingual:section -->

## 超过 90% 不是写代码 / Over 90% isn't coding

<!-- bilingual:section -->

<!-- lang:zh -->

从共同撰写一篇帖子，到对一整套客户发现通话记录进行深入研究，人们使用 Cowork 的方式令人意外。

我们对人们委托的所有任务进行了分类，绝大多数都不是写代码。事实上，超过 90% 是知识工作——文档、分析、内容创作和沟通。

<!-- lang:en -->

From cowriting a post to doing deep research across a set of customer discovery call transcripts, people have used Cowork in surprising ways.

We categorized all the tasks people delegate to Cowork, and the vast majority aren't coding. In fact, more than 90% is knowledge work — documents, analysis, content creation, and communication.

<!-- /bilingual:section -->

### 人们在委托的业务职能 / The business functions people are delegating

<!-- bilingual:section -->

<!-- lang:zh -->

Cowork 中被委托任务最多的职能包括：

- 从通话记录中构建客户成功案例研究
- 为下周的管道审查做好准备——“把所有方框连起来”
- 审查合同，包括将大数额拆解为可消化的组成部分
- 自动开展研究，制作每日和每周的行业简报

大多数任务（用户称之为“委托”）都是由 Claude 协同用户连接的数十种工具完成的：日历、Google Workspace、Slack、Salesforce 等等。这些不是聊天，而是真正的 agent 行为：Claude 自主使用工具、读写文件，或者像真人同事一样查找事实。

<!-- lang:en -->

The top functions where tasks get delegated in Cowork include building case studies from call transcripts, getting ready for next week's pipeline review by "connecting all the boxes," contract review that breaks big numbers into consumable building blocks, and automated research for daily and weekly industry briefs.

Most tasks—which they call delegations—are completed by Claude working across dozens of tools the user has connected: calendar, Google Workspace, Slack, Salesforce, and more. These aren't chats. They're true agent behavior: Claude using tools on its own, reading and writing files, looking up facts the way a human colleague would.

## 什么样的委托任务效果最好 / What makes a good delegation

<!-- bilingual:section -->

<!-- lang:zh -->

那么，什么样的工作适合委托给 agent？我们回顾了人们交给 Cowork 的数万条任务，发现成功的任务呈现出非常清晰的形态：委托通常以结果为导向（“给我 X”），但建立在灵活的约束条件之上（“根据当前管道，使用最合适的数据”）。

委托任务具有自然边界，而不是一次性进行大量读取；Claude 可以围绕明确的里程碑规划路径：读取这些文件，检查这个来源，带着初步发现回来供你审阅，然后完成最终输出。

人们会注意把合适的工作交给 Claude：判断和依赖丰富上下文的活动由人完成，耗时的机械工作则交给 Claude。

<!-- lang:en -->

So what kind of work gets delegated? We looked back at tens of thousands of tasks people handed to Cowork, and what's landed has a very clear shape. A delegation is generally output-oriented ("get me X") but set against flexible constraints ("use whatever is best based on current pipeline data").

Delegations have natural boundaries instead of one massive read — concrete milestones Claude can plan a path to: read these files, check this source, come back with a preliminary finding for you to review, then do the final output.

People are careful to give Claude the right work: judgment, context-rich activities vs. time-intensive mechanical work. Humans do the former, Claude does the latter.

<!-- /bilingual:section -->

## 委托正在加速 / Delegation is accelerating

<!-- bilingual:section -->

<!-- lang:zh -->

人们适应了“委托—写作—审阅—再委托”的节奏后，开始找到自己的工作节拍：

- 在一天或一周开始时，先委托 Claude 构建一份简报包：会议笔记、邮件草稿，以及他们需要的其他内容。
- 将更多从想法到初稿的阶段外包出去。
- 大多数人从简单的小任务开始，逐步培养协同工作的直觉，然后转向更大的任务。

按人均计算，六个月间委托量增加了 72 倍。部分原因在于信任逐步建立：人们看到了结果，也开始信任更大型的任务。部分原因在于 Cowork 本身变得更好了：上下文检索更快，对信息和协作的处理更出色，任务结束后的总结也更好。

<!-- lang:en -->

As people settle into the rhythm of delegate-write-review-delegate, they find their own cadence:
Starting their day or week by delegating Claude to build a briefing pack: meeting notes, email drafts, and whatever else they need.
Outsourcing more of the ideation-to-draft phase.
Starting with small, simple tasks, steadily building that muscle for collaborative work, then turning to bigger tasks.

Per-user delegation volume increased 72X over six months. Partly it's trust that's been built. People see results and trust bigger tasks. Partly it's that Cowork itself got better: faster context retrieval, better handling of information and collaboration, and better post-task summaries.

<!-- /bilingual:section -->

## 重度用户做对了什么 / What heavy users get right

<!-- bilingual:section -->

<!-- lang:zh -->

重度用户有一个共同的行为特征：他们会用对成功标准的精确定义来描述任务——例如“完成这篇对外传播稿的编辑（要好的那种）”——这表明他们对“完成”应该是什么样子，已经形成了明确直觉。

这群人倾向于把纯机械编辑或纯信息挖掘的工作外包给 Claude，同时保留那些需要人类品味、依赖判断的部分。

他们也更快摸清了“检查 Claude 的工作—纠正—引导—完成”的循环。他们不会只使用一次就停下来，而是持续迭代，形成每隔一到两轮就审阅一次并立即提供方向性反馈的节奏。

重度用户几乎从不让超过两轮交互过去而不回复。速度对这个循环至关重要：如果某一轮停留太久，上下文就会过期，节奏也会中断。让循环保持快速运转，就是秘诀。

重度用户常常会给 Claude 设立“约束沙箱”——“你不要处理演示文稿中的方框文案，只处理语言本身”——因为他们已经知道答案不在那些方框里。

而且，在打开 Cowork 后的两个小时内，最活跃的用户完成的工作量就超出了他们原本预期的上限。

<!-- lang:en -->

Across heavy users, there's a shared behavioral trait: they describe tasks with a precise definition of success — "done editing this external comms piece (the good kind)" — showing they already have a strong intuition for what "finished" should look like.

This group tends to outsource purely mechanical edits or purely information-mining stuff to Claude while keeping the judgment-intensive parts that require human taste.

They also figured out the loop of checking Claude's work, correcting, steering, and finishing faster. They don't just use it once and stop; they iterate, establishing a cadence of reviewing and giving immediate directional feedback with every one or two turn.

Power users almost never let more than two turns go by without a reply. Speed matters for this loop: if a turn sits for too long, context expires and rhythm breaks. Keeping the loop moving is the secret.

Heavy users often give Claude a "constraint sandbox" — "you don't control the box copy in the deck, please just touch the language" — when they already know that the answer isn't in those boxes.

And, in two hours after they open it, most active people get more done than they ever expected.

## 那么，工作会变成什么样？ / So what does work become?

<!-- bilingual:section -->

<!-- lang:zh -->

如果你把工作中不需要你的部分交给 agent，把需要你的部分留给自己，工作会开始变成这样：早上 8 点，你拿起手机，Claude 已经制作好一份简报文档，总结了昨天 Slack 中所有相关项目的更新，附上邮件草稿，并整理好当天下午客户会议的计划。你花十分钟评论通话记录稿，Claude 接手给出建议，然后你去吃早餐。

这个观念本身并不新。在此之前，大多数知识工作者只是缺少合适的工具来将它实现。Cowork 正在让它成为现实，并承担起人们在工作中处于被动响应状态时所消耗的越来越多时间。

Cowork 仍处于早期阶段。规模化带来了新的问题——安全、控制、工作存放的位置，以及如何审计那些被无数无形之手触碰过的工作——这些都是我们会谨慎处理并稳步推出方案的领域，不需要大肆喧哗。

<!-- lang:en -->

If you give the agent the parts of your work that don't need you, and keep the parts that do, work starts to look like this: at 8 a.m., you grab your phone, and Claude has already built a briefing doc with the relevant Slack project updates from yesterday, email drafts attached, and a plan for your client meeting this afternoon. You spend ten minutes commenting on the call transcript, Claude takes over the recommendations, and you go grab breakfast.

The idea itself isn't new. Until now, most knowledge workers simply lacked the right tools to make it real. Cowork is making it real, and it's taking on a growing share of the hours most people spend in reactive mode at their roles.

Cowork is still in its early stages. Scale brings new questions — security, control, where the work lives, and how you audit work that dozens of unseen hands have touched — areas we're holding carefully and will ship steadily, without needing to be loud.
